The economics of scale

The pieces for 5,000 people cost nearly what the foundation costs for our full knowledge-worker base.

4×the coverage, ~$800K more
Assembled separately, Copilot, Agent 365, and Entra Suite reach roughly $3.08M a year for 5,000 people. The E7 foundation delivers the same three capabilities to all ~20,000 eligible knowledge workers (E5) for about $3.8M in year one. Four times the coverage, fully bundled and governed, for about $800K more. A tailored model built with Microsoft, not off-the-shelf pricing.

Why the per-user price falls

Bought individually, the stack is $50.75 per user per month. As one foundation it is about $16 in year one, because the licensed layers are bundled rather than priced as separate add-ons. The decision is not Copilot versus E7. It is three products, bought separately and later, versus one foundation that includes them, priced for our whole eligible knowledge-worker base. Agent consumption sits on top of both and is where the real scale question lives, addressed on page 2.

Acting early locks the price

Purchasing before the increase, for example in December, locks year-one pricing through May 1 and then for a further 12 months. That gives us room to ramp adoption while holding the best rate, so more of our knowledge workers are on the foundation before pricing steps up.

Consumption at scale

Licences are the fixed part. Consumption is where scale decides the cost.

Page 1 compared the licensed layers. Two forces grow on top of them: covering all ~20,000 eligible knowledge workers (E5), and agent consumption that rises as the estate moves from a single agent to many. This is where the piecemeal path becomes expensive and hard to forecast.

One agent was $36K a year. Left ungoverned, consumption is the line item that grows fastest and is hardest to forecast. On a foundation it is metered, budgeted, and reserved for differentiated work.

3 Managing consumption

First: the strategy
  • Tier the work. Foundation-covered (fixed) for everyday scenarios; metered only for differentiated, autonomous, external, and advanced workloads.
  • Decide how, when, and why to consume before scaling, not after.
  • One standard for identity, governance, and observability across every agent.
Then: the mechanics
  • Forecast: per-workload consumption telemetry.
  • Budget: caps by department and workload, with clear ownership.
  • Govern: approval gates before a workload meters.
  • Optimize: model routing, right-sizing, and retiring waste.

4 Three-year total cost of ownership DirectionalRamped pricing

This is a ramp, not a flat buy. The foundation starts at $16/user/mo in year one and blends to about $20/user/mo across three years as pricing steps up and coverage grows. Assembling the SKUs separately blends to about $50/user/mo. Directional; the defensible core is the per-component pricing on page 1.
