The economic question, in two parts

What AI costs is one question. How we manage it, and when we invest, are the real ones.

Managing the cost

How do we forecast, govern, and control consumption as AI scales, so cost stays predictable instead of climbing agent by agent?

The timing of the foundation

Do we invest in the foundation now to accelerate our AI transformation and execute strategy faster, rather than after enterprise scale has already arrived?

This page answers both, at planning assumptions we replace with our own telemetry before we commit.

$5.70MCopilot alone, 20,000
Licensing Copilot on its own now runs $23.75 per user per month, about $5.70M a year for 20,000 people. The E7 foundation is $20.00 per user per month, about $4.80M, and E7 also includes the agent control plane (Agent 365) and identity governance (Entra). Broad Copilot alone already costs more than the foundation that contains it.

On the $5.70M: we use list pricing as a directional example. At enterprise scale we would expect additional volume discounts, so standalone Copilot would likely land below $5.70M. The relationship is the point: the Copilot add-on is priced above the E7 step-up, and E7 bundles more.

Being honest about timing

E7 is not cheaper on day one. Today, our consumption, Copilot licenses, and control-plane spend do not yet add up to the E7 investment, and the crossover arrives further down the road, not on a straight line. The case for acting now rests on two costs of waiting: the rising, unpredictable spend we take on in the meantime, and the strategic execution we defer by scaling AI without a foundation.

Managing consumption

Consumption happens on both paths. The foundation is where we govern it.

Consumption is not eliminated by E7, and we do not claim it is. The question the board is really asking is how we forecast and control it. That starts with strategy, then mechanics.

Left ungoverned, this is the line item that grows fastest and is hardest to forecast. On a foundation, it is metered, budgeted, and reserved for differentiated work.

4 How we manage consumption

First: the strategy
  • Tier the work. Foundation-covered (fixed) for everyday scenarios; metered only for differentiated, autonomous, external, and advanced workloads.
  • Decide how, when, and why we consume before we scale, not after.
  • One standard for identity, governance, and observability across all agents.
Then: the mechanics
  • Forecast: per-workload consumption telemetry.
  • Budget: caps by department and workload, with ownership.
  • Govern: approval gates before a workload meters.
  • Optimize: model routing, right-sizing, and retiring waste.
